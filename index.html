AI and chip stocks drive the S&P 500 into correction and its worst week since April 2025

▼ Bearish, high impact

The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.3% on Friday Nov 20 and the S&P 500 fell 3.7%, with the Dow down 1.9%. For the week the S&P 500 lost 8.6%, the Dow about 8% and the Nasdaq about 10%. Nvidia fell about 14% on Friday, and the VIX closed at its highest level since April 2025.

Why it matters: Tech and chip stocks carry a large share of index weight, so their selloff drags the whole market down. Investors are also questioning whether heavy AI spending will earn enough profit to justify current valuations.

Futures put about 91% odds on a December Fed rate hike, with some officials pushing for a bigger move

▼ Bearish, high impact

Fed funds futures priced roughly 91% odds of a quarter-point hike at the Dec 8 to 9 meeting as of Nov 22. Governor Waller has floated a half-point hike in December, a larger move that markets had not priced in. Minutes from the October meeting showed nearly all officials expect to raise rates next month.

  • S&P 500
  • Nasdaq Composite
  • Russell 2000
  • Rate-sensitive growth stocks
  • Banks and homebuilders
  • US Treasuries
Why it matters

Why it matters: Higher expected rates raise borrowing costs and lower the present value of future earnings, which hits high-valuation growth stocks hardest. Small caps, which borrow heavily, are also sensitive, and the Russell 2000 fell 2.8% on Nov 20.

October core PCE inflation and Q3 GDP revision land Wednesday, the last major data before the Fed's blackout

Neutral, high impact

October core PCE, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, is due Wednesday Nov 25, with forecasts clustering around 3.2% year over year. Q3 GDP is revised the same day, and some economists expect growth to be revised up to about 3.6% from the advance estimate of 3.4%. The Fed's pre-meeting quiet period starts Saturday Nov 28.

  • S&P 500
  • Nasdaq Composite
  • US Treasuries
  • Dollar
Why it matters

Why it matters: A hot PCE print would strengthen the case for a December hike and could push yields and tech stocks lower. A soft print would ease pressure, though one analysis notes it would not cancel a hike.

Bottom line

US stocks end a painful week with the S&P 500 down 8.6% and the Nasdaq down about 10%, led by AI and chip stocks. Futures put about 91% odds on a December Fed hike, so the market now treats higher rates as the base case rather than a risk. The holiday-shortened week hinges on Wednesday's October PCE print, and that data will set the tone into the Dec 8 to 9 Fed meeting.
