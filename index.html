AI and chip stocks sell off hard on Thursday, Nov 5

▼ Bearish, high impact

The S&P 500 fell 4.36% and the Dow lost 3.44%, both their worst sessions since April 2025. The Nasdaq fell about 5.5%, pushed into correction territory. Nvidia dropped about 9%, AMD about 17% and Micron about 17.5%. Sources differ on exact percentages, so treat the figures as approximate.

Why it matters: The AI trade is the main growth driver of the index, so doubts about how fast data center and chip spending will pay off hit the largest index weights. Reports also cite concerns about regulatory limits and the financing cost of the AI buildout.

October payrolls beat forecasts at 165,000 and unemployment falls to 4.1%

▼ Bearish, high impact

The October jobs report showed 165,000 new jobs against roughly 62,000 to 65,000 expected. The unemployment rate fell to 4.1% and August and September were revised up by a combined 84,000. Manufacturing added 19,000 jobs.

  • Treasury yields
  • US dollar
  • rate-sensitive growth stocks
  • Nasdaq
  • S&P 500
Why it matters

Why it matters: A strong labor market makes the Fed less likely to cut and more open to another hike, which lifts yields and the dollar and pressures valuations for long-duration tech. The dollar index rose 0.7% on the day and the 10-year yield was about 4.86%.

Bottom line

US stocks are in a fragile spot after the worst AI-led sell-off since April 2025 and a Friday bounce that was much smaller. Strong October payrolls and a hawkish Fed tone keep yields near 4.86% and the dollar firm, which weighs on richly valued tech. This week's CPI print and the AI-linked earnings from CoreWeave, Cisco and Applied Materials will decide whether the selling was a reset or the start of a deeper correction.
