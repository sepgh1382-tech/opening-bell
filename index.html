Midterm results trigger the worst S&P 500 week since April 2025

▼ Bearish, high impact

The S&P 500 fell 2.7% for the week to 7,376.86, its fourth straight weekly loss, after a 4.36% plunge on Thursday. The Nasdaq entered a correction and the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index lost about 12% for the week.

Why it matters: Democratic wins raised fears of tighter oversight and local halts on data center construction, threatening the AI spending boom that has driven most of this year's gains. The Magnificent Seven lost about $1 trillion in value on Thursday alone.

Hot October jobs report pushes December Fed hike odds near 80%

▼ Bearish, high impact

Employers added 165,000 jobs in October, far above forecasts, and unemployment eased to 4.1%. Traders raised the odds of a quarter-point hike in December to about 78%, and LSEG data puts it near 80%.

  • All indices
  • Treasuries
  • Growth stocks
  • US dollar
Why it matters

Why it matters: A tighter Fed raises borrowing costs and pressures stock valuations, especially richly priced tech. The dollar firmed after the report.

October CPI on Tuesday is the week's big test

Neutral, high impact

The October consumer price report is due Tuesday at 8:30 a.m. ET, with inflation expected to rise to 3.9% year over year from 3.7%. Producer prices follow on Friday, and about 10 Fed officials speak during the week.

  • All indices
  • Treasuries
  • Rate-sensitive sectors
Why it matters

Why it matters: A hot reading would all but lock in a December hike and could extend the selloff, while a soft one could fuel a relief rally.

Data center opponents gain power after the elections

▼ Bearish, medium impact

Democrats who campaigned partly on data center moratoriums won governor races in Texas and Georgia, and voters in dozens of local areas rejected or restricted projects. Goldman Sachs estimated temporary halts could affect about 45% of planned US data center expansion.

  • Data centers

  • NVDA

Why it matters

Why it matters: Delays to data center builds would slow orders for chips, power and equipment. HSBC expects tighter oversight rather than broad moratoriums, and Goldman sees the effect as short-lived.

Bottom line

Last week broke the AI trade's momentum: the election raised real doubts about data center growth, and a strong jobs report revived the threat of a December rate hike. Falling oil and slightly lower yields are small cushions. Tuesday's CPI report is the key event, since a hot number would deepen rate fears while a cool one could spark a relief rally. Expect elevated volatility until CPI and Nvidia's results on November 17 give clearer signals.
