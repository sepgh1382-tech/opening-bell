AI and chip stocks suffer the worst sell-off since April 2025 on Thu Nov 5

▼ Bearish, high impact

The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.52%, the S&P 500 dropped about 4.4% to 7,353.73 and the Dow lost about 3.44%, or more than 1,800 points. Reuters estimated about $3 trillion of value was wiped from S&P 500 companies. The Nasdaq is now nearly 12% below its Oct 6 record.

Why it matters: AI and chip companies carry large weights in the major indexes, so doubts about data center spending and tighter AI rules hit the whole market. Reports tie the fear to the midterm results, with Democrats winning control of both chambers of Congress.

October payrolls beat forecasts with 165,000 jobs and unemployment at 4.1%

▼ Bearish, high impact

The Bureau of Labor Statistics reported 165,000 new jobs in October, against forecasts of roughly 59,000 to 65,000. The unemployment rate fell 0.1 point to 4.1%, hourly wages rose 3.1% from a year earlier, and September was revised up to 60,000.

  • Treasury yields
  • US dollar
  • rate-sensitive growth stocks
  • Nasdaq
  • S&P 500
Why it matters

Why it matters: A strong labor market makes the Fed less likely to cut and more open to another hike. That lifts yields and the dollar, and it weighs on long-duration tech stocks.

Bottom line

US stocks are in a fragile spot after the sharpest AI-led sell-off since April 2025 and a Friday bounce that recovered only a small part of it. Strong October payrolls and a hawkish Fed tone have pushed the odds of a December hike to around 70% to 80%, while the 10-year yield sits near 4.86%. Today's CPI print and the AI-linked earnings this week will show whether the selling was a reset or the start of a deeper correction.
